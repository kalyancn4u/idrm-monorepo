> A fitting finish to the library: it connects monitoring, incidents, DR, and testing into one practice.*

---

## 1. What SRE is

**SRE (Site Reliability Engineering)** is Google's approach to operations: treat **reliability as an engineering
problem**. Instead of a separate ops team firefighting, engineers use software, measurement, and automation to
keep services dependable — and to decide, with data, *how* reliable a service needs to be.

Its core insight: **100% reliability is the wrong target** — it's impossibly expensive and users can't tell the
difference. So you choose a realistic goal and manage to it.

---

## 2. The core vocabulary

- **SLI (Service Level Indicator)** — a measured signal of health (e.g. % of requests under 500 ms, % successful).
- **SLO (Service Level Objective)** — the **target** for an SLI (e.g. "99.9% of requests succeed"). Your definition
  of "reliable enough."
- **SLA (Service Level Agreement)** — a *contractual* promise to users, usually looser than the internal SLO.
- **Error budget** — the allowed amount of unreliability (100% − SLO). If your SLO is 99.9%, you have 0.1% to
  "spend." Under budget → ship features; budget exhausted → stop and fix reliability. It turns "fast vs stable"
  from an argument into a **number**.

## 4. IDRM context

- **MVP:** the *mindset* without the machinery — define what "healthy" means (basic SLIs from the metrics
  endpoints), keep runbooks, automate the setup, and review incidents. Lean but principled.
- **FFP:** full SRE — formal SLOs/error budgets per service, on-call rotations, deep observability, autoscaling,
  and reliability engineered across the distributed system. See [`../../instructions/observability.md`](../../instructions/observability.md).

> For IDRM the stakes are literal: reliability isn't a nicety, it's whether help reaches people during a disaster.
