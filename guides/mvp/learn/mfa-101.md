# MFA 101

> *Type: Guide (101 / foundational) · Audience: all stakeholders → developers · Status: **FFP** — next-phase · Track: Security (#21)*
> *Passwords get stolen, guessed, and reused. Multi-Factor Authentication adds a second lock so a stolen password
> isn't enough. Read [IAM 101](iam-101.md) first. MFA is an **FFP** addition to IDRM.*

---

## 1. What MFA is

**MFA (Multi-Factor Authentication)** requires **two or more different kinds of proof** to log in, drawn from
distinct categories:

- **Something you know** — a password or PIN.
- **Something you have** — a phone, an authenticator app, a hardware key.
- **Something you are** — a fingerprint or face (biometrics).

The power is in **combining categories**: a stolen password (know) is useless without the phone (have). Two
passwords wouldn't count — same category. **2FA** (two-factor) is MFA with exactly two.

---

## 2. Common second factors

- **TOTP** (Time-based One-Time Password) — the rotating 6-digit code in apps like Google Authenticator. Simple,
  offline, widely used.
- **Push approval** — a "was this you?" prompt on your phone.
- **SMS codes** — convenient but the weakest (SIM-swap/interception risk); avoid for high-value accounts.
- **Hardware security keys** (FIDO2/WebAuthn) — the strongest, phishing-resistant option.

---

## 3. Why it matters

The vast majority of account breaches start with a compromised password. MFA blocks most of them outright,
because the attacker rarely has your *second* factor. For IDRM, high-privilege accounts (**coordinator/admin** —
who approve and verify life-critical incidents) are exactly the ones worth protecting this way.

---

## 4. IDRM's phasing

- **MVP:** single-factor — a strong, bcrypt-hashed password with lockout ([IAM 101](iam-101.md)). Deliberately
  simple.
- **FFP:** MFA, especially for privileged roles, typically delivered through the IdP alongside OIDC/OAuth
  ([OIDC 101](oidc-101.md), [OAuth 2.1 + PKCE 101](oauth-2.1-pkce-101.md)). See
  [`../../instructions/security.md`](../../../archive/instructions/security.md).

> Note: IDRM's face **detection** (an upload quality check) is **not** biometric authentication — it identifies
> nobody. Biometric MFA is a separate, consented, FFP matter under the DPDP Act
> ([Secure Coding 101](secure-coding-101.md)).
